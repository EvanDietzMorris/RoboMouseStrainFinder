What makes the answer trustworthy

You pin the term, not the guess

Type-ahead resolves against NameRes. Picking a suggestion pins that CURIE, so the traversal starts from the concept you meant rather than the top-scoring match.

Curated predicates only

Genes are collected through association predicates — has_phenotype, causes, model_of and friends. related_to and target_for are excluded on purpose: co-occurrence and drug-target semantics are not implication.

Cross-species by construction

UPheno bridges HP↔MP and MONDO→MP; Alliance orthology bridges human↔mouse genes. Ask in human and you still land on mouse stocks. Turn either bridge off to see the unexpanded result.

Every hit carries its receipts

Each gene reports the predicates and primary knowledge sources that produced it. Each response carries the graph version, catalog date and retrieval time.

Shareable and exportable

Term, vocabulary and pinned CURIE live in the query string, so a result is a link you can paste to a collaborator. The full strain table exports to CSV.

Same API underneath

The page calls nothing private — /api/resolve and /api/search are the same endpoints your notebook can hit. No build step, no framework, no server-rendered results.

Ranked by share of the stock, not by hit count

The MMRRC catalog is dominated by the Missouri ENU mutagenesis archive, where a single stock can carry eighty-odd incidental gene annotations. Rank by “number of query genes matched” and those lines bury every targeted knockout.

So the ranking is matched ÷ annotated. Both numbers come back with the result, so you can audit the order — and one checkbox drops the chemically-induced lines entirely.

Read this before you order anything

A returned strain is a gene-level candidate. It is annotated with a gene the knowledge graph associates with your phenotype — that is not a claim that the stock's allele reproduces the phenotype. Confirm allele, zygosity and genetic background against the MMRRC strain detail sheet and the originating publication. Gene-level similarity is not allele equivalence.
